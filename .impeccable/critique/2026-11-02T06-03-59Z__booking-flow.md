# Assessment A: Design Review, setter booking flow (after M3.5 build)

- **Target:** the setter booking sheet (`components/booking/booking-sheet.tsx` and `sheet-screens.tsx`), opened from the Home and Schedule time tiles, through "Existing lead instead", and from a lead page's "Book a meeting".
- **Evidence:** 21 phone captures (390 x 664 visible, 2x), 10 iPad captures (1194 x 834, landscape), the two scroll records, and the source files named in the brief.
- **Method:** Assessment A only, read-only and isolated. No browser, server, detector or database, and no earlier scores or detector output read.

**Run notes**
- The captures were taken on Sunday, Nov 1, 2026, about 10 pm Calgary time, so "Tonight: No times" is correct. TEST names, the grey photo fixture and Keenan's meeting times are not judged.
- **Scroll records:** none of the nine step screens scrolls, on the phone or the iPad. The time pickers inside the sheet do scroll on a phone, with the third row of tiles under the bar. That is reasonable for a picker, and those screens are not in the records.
- **States with no capture:** a lapsed hold, a failed upload, Book with items missing, and a distance warning. Findings about these come from the source and say so.

## Design specificity verdict

**Authored for Vesper in its structure and its words. Category-standard in its visual frame.**

This flow could only belong to Vesper:
- **The hold is the spine of the interface.** A chip on every screen reads "Sat, Nov 14 at 5:00 p.m. held for you", a line the setter can turn and show. Times other people are booking carry the holder's initials. "Let go of 6:45 p.m.?" spells out the consequence, and the done screen names the evening.
- **The steps are the door process itself:** At the door, After the door, Book. The "?" carries Keenan's own words for each step.
- **The helper copy is Vesper's.** The photo slots say how to frame a roofline and what to do at night. The soffit question explains why off-white matters. "Use where I'm standing" reports its accuracy in metres.
- **Before you book is a visible gate,** not hidden validation.

Swap in another company and most of the copy stops making sense. That is the test, and this flow passes it.

The frame is the generic part:
- **Without the words, it is a standard dark-mode stepper:** a three-segment bar on top, one centred column of outlined fields and buttons, and Back and Next in a footer.
- **The signature component is missing from the steps.** The time tile, the product's one signature component, appears only on the times screens. Inside the steps, the brand is a gold-bordered chip and a gold button.
- **The Porch Light Rule holds inside the sheet, with real discipline.** Chosen answers are cream with a tick, and gold is only the held time and the next action. No template would do that.
- **Outside the sheet, the rule breaks.** Home and the in-sheet picker put six gold-outlined Open tiles on one screen. The rule's own test ("if two things on a screen are gold, one of them is wrong") flags this. The tension comes from DESIGN.md's time-tile spec, not from this build.

Missed chances for character:
- **The moment of commitment is under-marked.** The homeowner has just said yes and is looking at the screen. They see a 14 px chip in the top left and a 24 px question addressed to the setter ("Who are we meeting?"). DESIGN.md already defines a Time style: 24 px bold, tabular figures. Showing the held time at that size on the first screen, then letting it settle into the chip, would make "it's yours" readable at arm's length.
- **The iPad gets the phone sheet, centred.** At 1194 px, the 640 px column leaves about 280 px of empty navy on each side. Nothing the homeowner reads (the time, the chip) gets bigger.
- **The done screen speaks only to the setter.** The spec says the setter confirms the time with the customer at the door. When the setter finishes on the sidewalk, the screen gives them nothing to say back.

**Deterministic scan:** not part of Assessment A.

## Cognitive load

- [x] **Single focus:** one step's question per screen, and nothing competes with it.
- [x] **Chunking:** at most three questions per screen and four answers per question. The exception is Before you book: seven rows in one block.
- [x] **Grouping:** one fieldset per question. The quiet row and the Back/Next row read as two groups.
- [x] **Visual hierarchy:** a heading, then the answers, then one gold Next.
- [x] **One thing at a time:** nothing auto-advances. Next is the setter's call.
- [ ] **Minimal choices:** five actions sit on every step screen: "?", Pick another time, Let go, Back and Next. The quiet pair is the same white semibold as the question labels. The spec requires all five, so the fix is visual weight, not removal.
- [ ] **Working memory:** after Existing lead, the setter has to remember whose booking it is, because no name shows until Book. Pick another time makes them find the held evening again. A Missing "Owns the home: Yes" row doesn't say what was answered.
- [x] **Progressive disclosure:** the soffit note shows only on Other, the photo skip reasons only behind "Can't get the photos?", and the notes behind "?".

**Result: 2 failures, moderate load.** The task itself is well sequenced: seven answers, an address, three photos and a confirmation. The extra load comes from orientation and memory.

**Decision points with more than 4 visible options:**
- **Every step screen:** five chrome actions plus the screen's own answers. Find out part 2 shows 10 answer buttons plus the five, so 15 targets.
- **Times inside the sheet** (Pick another time, lead-first, Move): a strip of up to 15 evenings (about 4 visible on a phone, 7 on the iPad) and up to 12 time tiles. This comes with choosing a time, and opening on the wrong evening makes it worse.
- **Existing lead:** up to 50 leads, newest first, with no search.
- **Book with items missing:** one Fix button per missing row, up to seven, plus the chrome.
- **Before you book:** seven status rows in one list. Grouping them by the step that fixes them would chunk it as 4 + 3 and teach the step order. At the door: phone, owns the home, wants a price, everyone who decides. After the door: map position, soffit, photos.

## Emotional journey

1. **The tap (the peak).** The sheet covers the screen at once and the time is claimed. "held for you" is the right phrase. But the commitment is a small chip, and the loudest thing on screen is a question for the setter, so the homeowner's moment is under-marked.
2. **At the door.** One-tap answers keep the setter's eyes up, and "?" holds Keenan's script if they blank.
   - Valley: when the homeowner says they rent, or don't want a price yet, the screen doesn't react. The setter learns it matters only at Book, after the address and photos.
3. **The pocket moment.** The setter is talking and the phone is idle, so it auto-locks. After the grace period the hold lets go. The next thing on screen is a red chip ("not held") and a red-bordered notice.
   - This is the deepest valley, and it can land with the homeowner watching. (From the source; not captured.)
4. **After the door, on the sidewalk.** Practical and reassuring: "Map position from this phone at the door, accurate to about 10 m", and framing tips on each photo slot.
   - Valley on weak signal: while photos upload, Next reads "Saving", and Back, Pick another time and Let go are all disabled.
5. **Book (the high-stakes moment).** Reassurance done right: seven ticks, and a button that names the outcome ("Book 5:00 p.m."). With something missing, the disabled "Fix 2 first" is clear but terse.
6. **The end.** "Booked for Sat, Nov 14 at 5:00 p.m.", and the chip turns to a tick and "booked". Calm, specific and on brand. It closes the loop for the setter, not for the homeowner.
7. **Letting go.** The dialog states the consequence: "stays saved as a lead. Anyone can then pick this time." That is good reassurance at a destructive moment.

**Peak-end:** the end is good. The peak, the tap, is under-designed, and the worst valley, a red lapse, can happen in the homeowner's view.

## What's working

1. **The hold is something the setter can say out loud.** A database rule became a visible promise:
   - a chip on every screen that says "held for you";
   - holder initials on times other people hold, with the board re-read every 3 seconds;
   - Pick another time keeps the current hold until a new time is tapped;
   - Escape or a phone's back gesture asks "Let go of 6:45 p.m.?" first;
   - on the done screen the chip flips to a tick and "booked", so the promise visibly closes.
2. **The flow follows the conversation instead of driving it.**
   - The steps are named after the door process, and Keenan's notes sit behind "?" for each step.
   - Answers take one tap and clear on a second. Nothing auto-advances, and nothing is required to move on.
   - All nine step screens fit a 390 x 664 phone with no scrolling; both scroll records are all false.
   - Helper lines are written for the moment: "Needed to confirm the meeting.", "White is the only stock track colour, so off-white matters. Not sure is fine.", "Ask who else decides on the house, and that they can be at the meeting."
3. **Before you book is an honest gate, with disciplined colour.**
   - Each item is ticked, or marked with a hollow circle and a Fix that jumps to the exact screen.
   - The primary button names the outcome, and status never depends on colour alone.
   - The Porch Light Rule holds inside the sheet: chosen answers are cream with a tick, and gold is only the held time and the next action.

## Priority issues

### [P1] The "it's yours" promise is easy to lose at the door, and losing it looks like an alarm
- **What:**
  - **Nothing keeps the screen awake while a time is held.** The sheet checks in every 20 s, but only while the screen is visible. A phone that auto-locks during the conversation stops checking in, and the hold lets go after the grace period.
  - **A lapse turns red.** The chip turns red ("not held") and a red-bordered notice appears (`HeldChip`, `sheet-hold-lost`). The shape brief says the chip "turns quiet", and DESIGN.md keeps Warning Red for errors.
  - **A reload after a lapse loses the draft.** If iOS reloads the app, `BookingSheetHost` discards the stored draft when `renewMyHold` no longer holds the time (`writeStored(userId, null)`), and the sheet doesn't come back. A name, phone and Find out answers typed before the address screen are gone, with no message.
  - **Let go is a near-miss from Next.** "Let go" sits on the right of the quiet row, 4 px above Next (`gap-1`). Its confirmation then puts a red "Let go" exactly where Next was.
- **Why it matters:**
  - The sheet's job (brief, section 2) is that the setter can say "it's yours" the moment the time is tapped.
  - PRODUCT.md names phones that lock and camera reloads as normal conditions.
  - A lapse that is silent until it turns red, a reload that drops the person, and two fast taps at the bottom right each break the one promise the setter makes out loud, sometimes in front of the homeowner.
- **Fix:**
  - Request a screen wake lock (`navigator.wakeLock.request("screen")`) while a time is held. Request it again on `visibilitychange`, release it on book, let go and close, and skip it quietly where it isn't supported.
  - Build the lapsed state the brief describes: the chip with a neutral border and "not held", the notice in neutral text, and "Hold it again" as the one gold action, since it is now the next step.
  - After a reload with a lapsed hold, keep the draft and reopen the sheet on the same screen in the lapsed state instead of discarding it. This goes past the spec's reload rule, so it needs Keenan's OK.
  - Swap the quiet row: "Let go" on the left, above Back, and "Pick another time" on the right, where a slip is harmless because it keeps the hold. Add 8 to 12 px between the two rows.
- **Suggested command:** /impeccable harden

### [P1] When the homeowner isn't ready, the sheet has no good way out, and the gate reads like a typo
- **What:**
  - **No answer disqualifies until Book.** A disqualifying answer (Owns the home: No, Wants a price: Not yet) gets no response on Find out. At Book it shows as a hollow circle beside "Owns the home: Yes" with a "Fix" button, and nothing says the answer was No. Fix lands on Find out with No selected, and one tap on Yes passes the gate.
  - **The Everyone screen has only a Yes.** It offers "Yes, everyone who decides will be there" and no answer for "the spouse can't come".
  - **No follow-up step.** The Book note tells the setter to "let the time go and set a follow-up day", but the sheet has nowhere to do that:
    - before the address screen, Let go throws away everything typed (the dialog says so: "What you've entered isn't saved yet");
    - after it, the lead stays New with no date, and the sheet closes to Schedule.
- **Why it matters:**
  - Before you book exists so every meeting is worth Keenan's drive (PRODUCT.md, principle 3). Calling a renter "Missing" with a "Fix" frames a business rule as a data-entry slip and invites flipping the answer.
  - "Not today" is a normal ending at a door. If it costs the details the setter just collected, setters will either lose the lead or game the gate.
- **Fix:**
  - On Book, separate "not answered" (hollow circle, Fix) from "answered No". For a No, show the answer and the rule in plain words ("Owns the home: No. Only homeowners can be booked.") with a quiet "Change answer" instead of a Fix.
  - Say the same thing on Find out, as one quiet line under the question, as soon as No or Not yet is tapped, so the setter knows before the address and photos.
  - In the Let go dialog, when the person isn't saved yet but has a first name, add "Save them first": it jumps to the address screen and lets go once the lead saves.
  - After letting go of a saved lead, offer "Open the lead", where the follow-up picker already is.
- **Suggested command:** /impeccable shape, then /impeccable clarify for the wording

### [P2] Inside a step, the setter can't tell where they are, or whose booking it is
- **What:**
  - **The step bar barely moves.** It has three segments and doesn't move across the four At the door screens or the three After the door screens.
  - **Same title twice.** Find out part 1 and part 2 have the same title.
  - **Step names don't jump.** They aren't buttons, although the brief (section 6) says they jump to that step's first screen.
  - **The bar empties on side screens.** On Pick another time and Existing lead, the bar empties completely (phone 14 and 16), as if the setter had started over.
  - **No name after Existing lead.** After "Existing lead instead" or a lead-first entry, the person's name appears on no step until Book (phone 15).
  - **Photos don't say which ones count.** Nothing says that Front, Left corner and Right corner are the three needed to book, and that Soffit close-up is an extra.
- **Why it matters:** at the door the homeowner is waiting, and "two more quick questions" is something the setter needs to be able to say. Booking the wrong existing lead is a real error, and nothing on screen would catch it.
- **Fix:**
  - Fill the current segment screen by screen (a quarter, a half, and so on), and add "2 of 4" to the step label.
  - Title the Find out screens by content ("About the home", "About the job"), or "Find out, 1 of 2".
  - Make each step label a 44 px button that jumps to its first screen.
  - Keep the current step marked while on the times or Existing lead screens.
  - Show "for Jane Smith" under the chip once a name is known.
  - Mark the three needed photo tiles "Needed to book", and the soffit tile as an extra.
- **Suggested command:** /impeccable clarify

### [P2] Field and checkbox edges are too faint for a phone outdoors
- **What:**
  - **The input edge is too faint in both themes.** At night, `--input` (#2f4958) on the background (#0c1b26) is about 1.8:1. By day, #c7d0d6 on white is about 1.6:1.
  - **It is the only cue in key places.** That edge alone marks:
    - the empty fields on "Who are we meeting?" and "Where's the house?";
    - the unselected answers;
    - the unchecked Everyone box, a 2 px #2f4958 square.
  - **Edges are untested.** The contrast test in decisions.md covers text pairs only, so edges have never been checked.
- **Why it matters:** WCAG 1.4.11 asks for 3:1 on the edges that identify an input or a checkbox's state. At dusk with the screen dimmed, or in low sun, an empty "Who" screen reads as a blank page with labels. The Everyone box, the one confirmation stored on the booking itself, nearly disappears.
- **Fix:**
  - Raise `--input` to at least 3:1 in both themes: about #5b7584 at night (about 3.6:1) and about #8495a0 by day (about 3.1:1).
  - Keep `--border` for dividers.
  - Give the unchecked Everyone box the stronger stroke.
  - Add edge pairs to the contrast test.
- **Suggested command:** /impeccable audit

### [P2] Secondary paths cost extra taps: changing the time, finding a lead, waiting on uploads
- **What:**
  - **Changing the time opens on the wrong evening.** Pick another time and Move open on tonight, or on the next evening with an opening, not on the evening being changed. Holding Sat, Nov 14, the picker opened on Tomorrow (phone 16, iPad 16), because the sheet passes no `initialDay` to `TimePicker`.
  - **The iPad strip is clipped.** On the iPad, the evening strip is cut off at the 640 px column ("Sat, No…") while about 280 px sits empty on each side.
  - **Existing lead is hard to search.** It lists the 50 newest New and Follow up leads, with no search, and not in the Leads tab's order (follow-ups due first). Yet revisiting a follow-up is the likeliest reason to use it at a door.
  - **Uploads lock the bar.** While photos upload, Next reads "Saving", and Back, Pick another time and Let go are disabled for as long as the signal takes.
- **Why it matters:** "Can we make it a bit later?" is a common question at the door. The setter should answer it with one tap, not by paging back through evenings while the homeowner watches.
- **Fix:**
  - Pass the held time's evening as `initialDay`, or the booked time's evening for Move.
  - On the iPad, let the evening strip run the sheet's full width.
  - Order Existing lead like Leads, and add a search box once the list passes a screenful.
  - While photos upload, label Next "Uploading photos", and keep Back and the quiet row available.
- **Suggested command:** /impeccable polish

## Persona red flags

**Casey (distracted mobile user): the setter at the door, phone in one hand**
- Puts the phone down to talk, and it auto-locks. Two minutes later the time is no longer held, and the screen she turns to the homeowner shows a red "not held" chip.
- If iOS reloads the app in that state, the sheet doesn't come back. The name and phone she typed at the door are gone, with no message.
- Aiming for the top of Next, she can catch "Let go" 4 px above it. Tapping where Next was then confirms Let go.
- On weak signal at the photos screen, every bottom control is disabled while uploads finish, and Next says "Saving" though she saved nothing.
- To move Thursday 6:45 to 7:20, she has to page the evening strip back to Thursday.
- What works for her: every control is at least 44 px, Next is at the bottom right, and no step screen scrolls.

**Jordan (first-timer): a setter on their first evening**
- On the first screen the "?" isn't there yet. It appears after the context loads: it is missing in both first-screen captures (phone 01 and iPad 01), and it is an unlabelled icon in the top corner.
- Two screens called "Find out", and a bar that doesn't move for four screens: "how many more before I can book?"
- The homeowner rents, and nothing happens until Book. There, "Owns the home: Yes" has a "Fix" button. Jordan taps Fix and sees No selected, and nothing says that No means "don't book". The Book note says to set a follow-up day, but the sheet has nowhere to do it.
- Four photo slots, and no hint that the soffit close-up is optional for booking.
- "Map position" in the checklist is the app's word, not theirs.

**Sam (accessibility): VoiceOver, low vision**
- What works: the answer groups are real radio buttons, focus moves to each screen's heading, the dialogs keep focus and close with Escape, the Everyone toggle uses `aria-pressed`, and the photo tiles name both the action and the slot.

- Field, answer and checkbox edges are below 3:1 in both themes (see the P2 issue above).
- The photo upload status ("Uploading", "Didn't upload. Tap to try again.") changes inside the tile with no live region, so a failure is silent.

**Pat, the homeowner in the doorway** (a project persona from PRODUCT.md's Users: looks at the setter's phone or iPad to pick a time, never logs in, needs to trust the time is theirs)
- After the tap, the only sign the time is theirs is a 14 px chip in the corner. The big text on screen is a question for the setter.
- On the iPad, the same 14 px chip floats on a 1194 px screen.
- If the hold lapses, the red chip and red notice are what Pat sees.
- The screen they just chose a time on turns into a list about them ("Owns the home", "Wants a price"). Keenan's own note says to make it "a conversation, not a survey". Nothing in the sheet marks the moment the screen becomes the setter's again.

## Minor observations

**Copy and labels**
- "What colour is the soffit?" is followed by a "Soffit colour" label, which repeats the question.
- The Book heading is "Ready to book?". The brief had "Book 7:30 pm tonight", which ties the heading to the time.
- The disabled Book button reads "Fix 2 first". The brief's "2 things missing", beside a disabled "Book 7:30 pm", reads more naturally.
- "Hold the phone sideways…" also shows on the iPad.
- For tomorrow's times, the chip reads the date ("Mon, Nov 2 at …", from `timePhrase`) while the evening strip says "Tomorrow". One wording for the same evening would help.
- The done heading breaks after "Sat," on a phone ("Booked for Sat, / Nov 14 at 5:00 p.m."). A non-breaking space would keep the date together.

**Layout and colour**
- "Existing lead instead" sits alone on a right-aligned row, which opens a gap between the heading and the fields. It could share the heading's row, or sit under the phone field.
- The quiet row ("Pick another time", "Let go") is white semibold 14 px, the same as the question labels. Muted text would make it quiet and still readable (#a9b6bf is 8.4:1 at night).
- The red "Let go" button uses Warning Red, which DESIGN.md keeps for errors only. The distance warning and the nearby-lead notice use gold borders, which the Porch Light Rule keeps for the next action. A neutral emphasis would respect both rules.
- The step bar's upcoming segments (#182b38 on #0c1b26) are close to invisible, so the bar reads as a white line that grows.

**Behaviour**
- From the source, not captured: "Didn't upload. Tap to try again." is 12 px text set not to shrink, inside a photo tile's label bar about 157 px wide on a phone. It likely clips, and pushes the slot name to nothing.
- The distance warning appears only on Book, after the homeowner has gone back inside. "Pick another time" at that point means knocking again.
- "This lead already has a meeting booked. Move that booking instead." offers only Close. It could offer Move.
- The note panel shows Keenan's "- " lines as plain text. A hanging indent would scan faster with no change to what he types.
- Existing lead rows show the status but not the follow-up date, which is what tells a setter which revisit this is.
- The fields on the Who screen set no `enterKeyHint`. Setting "next" on the name fields would let a thumb move from field to field without reaching up the screen.

## Questions to consider

- What if the first second after the tap belonged to the homeowner? The held time large, in the Time style, with "Held for you", before it settles into the chip and the setter's questions take over.
- What does "not today" look like inside the sheet? If the most common unhappy ending at a door costs the details the setter just collected, which way will setters bend: losing leads, or flipping answers?
- Should a No on Owns the home stop the flow at Find out, with the reason and a follow-up path, instead of turning up as a "Fix" at Book?
- Should the screen stay awake whenever a time is held? The grace period protects everyone else's evening. A wake lock would protect this setter's promise.
- Is the iPad sheet meant to be the phone sheet, centred? At a door with an iPad, what would the homeowner's half of the screen show?
- If the done screen gave the setter one line to say back ("Keenan will see you Saturday at 5:00 p.m."), would that close the loop the spec leaves to the setter?

## Coverage gaps (not findings)

**1. The light theme was not scanned.** Every browser context used `colorScheme: "dark"`.

- The only gold text in the app is the New lead tab label: components/app-tabs.tsx:81 adds `text-primary` to a 12 px `text-xs font-semibold` label (line 78), on `bg-background`.
- In the light theme that is #b99257 on #ffffff, a contrast of 2.87:1. WCAG AA needs 4.5:1 for 12 px text. A light-theme scan would very likely report `low-contrast` on:
  - phone Home (the tab bar);
  - iPad Home and New lead (the side rail).
- In the dark theme the same label is 6.09:1 and passes, which matches the empty results.
- DESIGN.md's own rule reads "Don't use gold for body text on a light background."
