## 1. Design specificity verdict

More of this now belongs only to Vesper's dusk meeting. The deck still opens on the wordmark over the homeowner's own street, and still turns "what does it look like by day?" into a line the customer can drag. The colour moment now happens at the table without leaving the deck. Keenan holds the RAL book to the soffit, picks from his own list by name on a full-screen chooser with no swatches, and the customer's initials confirm "the track colour we will order". The price waits for the first measured foot ("Adds up here as we measure.") instead of flashing the box price. A fresh open starts at the cover, and leaving takes two deliberate taps, so the internal app no longer surprises the room.

Some of it is still generic scaffolding:
- **Text pages** use the same headline-and-paragraphs template, lowered a little but still ending about halfway down the screen.
- **The cover** is still the placeholder text wordmark (source).
- **Your price** is still two-thirds stock form on the customer's side.

- **The new colour chooser** is the lead page's admin form moved into the deck, with a "Set the track colour" legend and a Save button at the bottom left. It isn't yet a moment designed for the table.

## 3. Cognitive load

For the homeowner, the deck is light, and two spikes from last round are gone. There is no $500 flash before anything is measured, and no trip through internal screens to settle the colour. The price screen still asks them to find the bill among about 20 identical boxes while the total moves with every keystroke, though it now starts from a blank instead of $500.

The colour moment is now a clear two steps: choose with the RAL book, then initial. It has one trap: tapping the chosen colour to agree with it clears it.

Keenan carries less hidden state than last round. The deck opens at the cover, and the colour can be set without leaving. He still has to remember that the chevrons skip the gallery, and which moves are hidden (double tap, tap to move the line).
