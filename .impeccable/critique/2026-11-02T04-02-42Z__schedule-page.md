# Assessment A: Design Review, Schedule tab (before redesign)

*Evidence: the six before-captures (setter phone and iPad on open, and on Sat Nov 14 with one booking; Keenan's phone and iPad on Sat Nov 14, plus full-page captures) and the source in `app/(app)/app/schedule/(day)/page.tsx`, `components/schedule/*` and `lib/schedule/board.ts`. No live browser. "(source)" marks behaviour read from code, not seen on a capture. Contrast figures are computed from the DESIGN.md tokens, not measured.*

## 1. Design specificity verdict

Half authored. The content and rules belong to Vesper, but the layout is a standard booking-app pattern. The words are clearly this business's own: "Tonight / Tomorrow · 6 open", "While you book, it is held for you and nobody else can pick it", "Nothing left tonight. This is the next evening with an opening.", holder initials, "Photos skipped: Homeowner said no", the straight-line distance from the previous meeting. Navy, gold and Manrope are applied flat and calm, with no gradients, glass or emoji. The layout, though, is the generic booking widget: a horizontal row of date chips over a grid of slot buttons, then a stack of bordered cards and a form of outline buttons. With the palette and copy swapped it could be a salon or clinic scheduler. The "Porch Light" idea doesn't come through. Gold is spread over every open tile, the selected chip, the New lead button and the tab marker instead of marking one next step. PRODUCT.md calls the evening the centre of the product, yet the screen never draws it as an evening (a sequence of meetings and drives), only as a set of interchangeable buttons.

## 3. Cognitive load

**Setter view: 3 of 8 fail (moderate).**
- Chunking: six equal tiles in one block, and 15 evenings in one unbroken strip with no week break.
- Minimal choices: see the decision points below.
- Working memory: their own booking is managed elsewhere, and "Closed" assumes they know the 30-minute cutoff.

**Keenan's view: 7 of 8 fail (high).** Only "one thing at a time" passes, because the outcome form shows reasons only for Not qualified and Cancel asks for confirmation.
- Single focus: open tiles, booking cards and the block form compete, and nothing on the page says "this is your evening".
- Chunking: as above, plus six block buttons in one row.
- Grouping: one booking is split between a dim tile in the grid and a card further down, with no link between them.
- Visual hierarchy: inverted. Call, Navigate, Move and Cancel booking share one outline weight, and "Block the whole evening" looks the same as "Block 5:00 p.m.".
- Minimal choices: see the decision points below.
- Working memory: he has to match each tile to its card, and on a deep link the evening is named only in the heading.
- Progressive disclosure: the block form is fully open under every evening, although blocking is rare.

**Decision points with more than 4 visible options:**
- Evening strip: 15 chips (Tonight plus 14). About 3.5 are visible on the phone and 10 on the iPad, all the same weight except the selected one.
- Time grid: 6 equal tiles per evening with the current settings.
- Blocked times: a reason field plus 6 identical outline buttons (the whole evening plus 5 single times).
- Keenan's booking card: 5 actions (lead link, Call, Navigate, Move, Cancel booking). Once the time starts, add 4 outcome choices plus Save.
- Not qualified reasons: the Settings list plus Other; the count depends on Settings.

## 4. Emotional journey

**Setter checking times at a door at dusk**
- **Opening:** the screen leads with a title and two lines of instructions. The times start 290-330 px down a 664 px visible area. Neutral, a little slow.
- **Reassurance:** if tonight is gone, the board says so and opens the next evening with an opening. The chip counts let them offer "Tuesday has 6 times". Confidence rises.
- **Peak:** big gold-bordered times. One tap holds the time ("Holding") and opens the booking, with the promise that nobody else can take it.
- **Valleys:**
  - A "Closed" time with no reason, while the homeowner watches.
  - A red "[Name] is booking this time." that looks like a failure in front of the homeowner.
  - On weak signal, the tapped tile stuck on "Holding", every other tile dead and no message, at the most anxious moment of the visit.
- **End:** the win appears as a greyed, disabled "Your booking" tile that looks like a blocked time. The visit ends flat when it should end on a small win: their lead is now on Keenan's evening.

**Keenan planning his evening**
- **Opening:** he reads an instruction written for setters. If tonight is fully booked or under way, the page opens on tomorrow with "Nothing left tonight. This is the next evening with an opening." (source). To him that reads like an empty evening, a moment of doubt.
- **The grid:** his bookings are the faintest things on screen and the empty times glow.
- **Peak:** on each card, one-tap "Call (403) 555-0177" and "Navigate", the reason photos were skipped and where the map pin came from. Practical tools for the road that he can trust.
- **Valley:** the evening has no visible shape.
  - Distances are one sentence per card, and nothing marks a leg over 10 km.
  - On the iPad it is one long column, with the right half of each card empty.
  - Other evenings show no photos, because photos are fetched for tonight only (source).
- **End:** the page ends on a form for blocking times, not on the evening. After a meeting, the outcome form inside the card is a clean close: four one-tap choices and Save.

## 5. Strengths

1. **States are words, never colour alone, at sizes that work outdoors.** Every tile states what it is: "Open", "Your booking", "Booked · neighbourhood · setter", "Blocked", "Closed", "[Name] is booking", "You're holding this". Screen-reader labels pair the time with the state. Targets are generous: 80 px tiles with 24 px bold tabular times, 56 px evening chips, 44 px buttons.
2. **An honest, live board.** The database decides who gets a time. The screen re-reads every 3 s and again when the phone wakes, regains focus or reconnects. A held time shows the holder's initials and explains itself when tapped. The board never leaves a setter on an evening with nothing open. The header copy promises exactly what the database enforces.
3. **Keenan's card puts the road first and guards risky actions.** Call and Navigate are one tap each. Cancel booking states its consequence ("The lead goes to Follow up for tomorrow") and offers "Keep it". The outcome form shows reasons only when needed and its note field reminds "never a price". The empty state says what will appear and how.

## 6. Priority issues

**[P1] Bookings are the faintest thing on the board, and tapping one does nothing.**
- **What:**
  - Booked tiles reuse the blocked/closed treatment (`bg-muted text-muted-foreground opacity-70`, disabled).
  - On Keenan's phone and iPad, "Booked · TEST (automated) · TEST Setter A" is the dimmest text on screen while five empty times glow gold. The details sit in a separate card list below, with no link from the tile.
  - On the setter's Sat Nov 14 capture, "Your booking" looks like a blocked time and can't be tapped, and Schedule offers no way to move or cancel it.
- **Why it matters:**
  - Keenan's evening is made of the bookings, and a setter's booking is their proof of work. Both are pushed into the background.
  - The detail line computes to about 4.2:1 in the dark theme and about 3.0:1 in the light theme (the one a setter picks in bright sun). Both are below 4.5:1 for 14 px text.
  - Disabled tiles also drop out of the keyboard order.
- **Fix:**
  - Give booked tiles their own full-contrast state: card surface, solid border, the time in Night Text, neighbourhood and setter in body text, no opacity.
  - Keep blocked and closed tiles muted, but at 4.5:1 or better in both themes.
  - Make every booked tile tappable. For Keenan it opens or scrolls to the booking, and on the iPad shows it in a detail pane. For a setter, "Your booking" opens the booking with Move and Cancel.
  - On Keenan's view, quiet the open times (plain outline) so the bookings lead.
- **Command:** /impeccable layout

**[P1] Keenan's Schedule opens on the wrong evening when tonight is full or under way.**
- **What (source):** with no `?day=` in the link, the page uses `pickEvening`, which opens tonight only while tonight still has an open time. On a fully booked evening, or once tonight's last open time has started (his cutoff is the start time), Keenan's Schedule tab opens on the next evening with an opening. It also shows the setters' note "Nothing left tonight. This is the next evening with an opening."
- **Why it matters:** this is exactly when he needs tonight's bookings, Call, Navigate and outcomes, often between meetings or in the car. The note reads to him as "nothing on tonight".
- **Fix:** for the owner, open on tonight whenever tonight has meeting times or bookings, until the evening ends. Fall back to the next evening only on a day off. Show the jump note to setters only.
- **Command:** /impeccable harden

**[P1] A hold lost to weak signal freezes the board, and a stale board looks live.**
- **What (source):**
  - `tap()` sets `pending`, waits for `holdTime` and clears `pending` afterwards, with no try/finally and no timeout. If the request fails or hangs, every tile stays disabled and the tapped one reads "Holding" until the page reloads, with no message.
  - Failed background reads are dropped silently, so the board keeps showing its last state with no sign that it is old.
- **Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md names weak signal and phones that lock as normal at the door. The setter's most important tap, with the homeowner waiting, is the one that has no recovery.
- **Fix:**
  - Clear `pending` in a finally block.
  - Add a client timeout of about 8 s with a plain message: "No signal. This time isn't held yet. Try again."
  - When reads fail, show a quiet line under the date ("Offline. Last updated 6:02 p.m.") and remove it once reads succeed.
- **Command:** /impeccable harden

**[P2] The labels misstate the evening.**
- **What:**
  - The chip "No meetings" marks an evening that has no meeting times (Sunday). It reads as "nothing booked".
  - "Full" appears whenever no time is open, including when the remaining times have merely closed. The board returns past times as "closed" (source), so later in the day evenings read "Full" with nothing booked.
  - A "Closed" tile gives no reason and no next step.
- **Why it matters:** setters repeat these words to homeowners, and Keenan's own rule is honest numbers.
- **Fix:**
  - "Day off" or "No times" for an evening without slots.
  - "Booked up" only when every time is booked or held; otherwise "Done" or the number of times left.
  - On a closed tile, say why, using the setting: "Booking closed 30 min before" or "Started".
- **Command:** /impeccable clarify

**[P2] Keenan's evening is not laid out for the iPad, and blocking is a separate form.**
- **What:**
  - In iPad landscape (1194 px) the page is the phone layout widened, in one column: a 3-column grid, then 992 px-wide cards with their content in the left third and only the status at the far right, then the Blocked times form. There are no two panes, though DESIGN.md allows them on the iPad.
  - The setter's iPad view leaves the lower 45% of the screen empty.
  - Blocking is a reason field over six identical outline buttons that repeat the times already on the board, and "Block the whole evening" runs on one tap with no confirm.
- **Why it matters:** the iPad is where Keenan plans. He should see the evening's shape at a glance (order, gaps, long drives), not scroll through a column of cards.
- **Fix:**
  - On the iPad, use two panes. On the left, the evening as a vertical run of times, with bookings prominent and legs over 10 km flagged between them. On the right, the selected booking's details, photos and actions.
  - Move blocking onto the time itself: the owner taps an open tile and chooses Book or Block.
  - Make "Block the whole evening" a secondary, confirmed action that asks for the reason after the choice.
  - Collapse the section when nothing is blocked.
- **Command:** /impeccable adapt, then /impeccable distill for the block controls

## 7. Persona red flags

**Casey (distracted mobile user): a setter, one-handed, at a door**
- The evening strip is the only way to change evening. It sits about 180-235 px from the top of the 664 px visible area, so it is at the top of the screen, out of easy thumb reach. Reaching anything past Wednesday also takes a horizontal swipe, while the other hand holds the door or a clipboard.
- The times start halfway down the first screen. The top half is header, "Schedule", two lines of instructions, the strip and the date.
- On weak signal, the board can freeze on "Holding" and gives no sign it is stale (priority issue 3).
- "Sign out" in the header submits on one tap (source), and a mis-tap means digging up the password Keenan texted.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent user)**
- Greyed tiles fail contrast: about 4.2:1 in the dark theme and about 3.0:1 in the light theme for the 14 px detail line (computed from the tokens with `opacity-70`).
- Booked, blocked and closed tiles are disabled buttons, so keyboard users tab straight past them and never learn what is booked.
- Focus is nearly invisible on gold. Chips and tiles use a 2 px gold ring with no offset, so the ring merges into the gold selected chip and the gold "You're holding this" tile. On open tiles it only thickens the gold border.
- Holds and bookings that arrive by polling change tiles with no live announcement, so a screen-reader user learns a time is taken only by tapping it. The holder's name is in the label, which is good.
- `text-destructive` red is used for "[Name] is booking this time.", which is an expected state, not an error.

**Keenan on his iPad planning the evening (project persona)**
- At 5:30 pm with tonight booked solid, Schedule opens on tomorrow with "Nothing left tonight" (source).
- On Sat Nov 14 the strip shows Tonight through Tue Nov 10 and no chip is selected. The evening appears only in the heading "Saturday, November 14" on both his captures, because nothing in the picker scrolls the selected chip into view (source).
- The booked tile reads "Booked · TEST (automated) · TEST Setter A" in the dimmest text on screen. To see the address he scrolls down to a 992 px card whose right half is empty.
- There is no route view. Each leg is a sentence on its card, and nothing marks one over the 10 km limit. Other evenings' cards have no photos.
- The page opens with an instruction meant for setters ("Tap an open time to start booking it...") and closes with a form where "Block the whole evening" sits beside "Block 5:00 p.m." at the same weight.

## 8. Minor observations

- The booked tile's detail wraps to three lines on the phone. On Keenan's capture the 5:35 tile is taller than the 5:00 tile beside it, so the first row is ragged.
- "Bookings, Saturday, November 14" repeats the "Saturday, November 14" heading about 240 px above it on the iPad.
- The strip clips "Wed, No…" on the phone and "Tue, Nov 10" on the iPad mid-chip, with no fade or arrow. The iPad has room for every evening in two rows.
- The block buttons are as wide as their text and wrap, which looks ragged on the phone. The single "Reason (optional)" field above them applies to whichever button is tapped next.
- The blocked list shows time ranges ("5:00 p.m. to 5:35 p.m.") where every other place shows start times.
- Call, Navigate, Move and Cancel booking are all outline buttons of one weight, and Cancel booking sits right beside Move.
- At scroll top, the raised New lead button overlaps the inner bottom corners of the 7:20 and 7:55 tiles by a few pixels (setter phone, on open). The `main` bottom padding clears it at scroll end.
- The two-line instruction is permanent and the same for both roles. Once a setter has learned it, it only takes up height.
- In the full-page captures the tab bar appears mid-page and the iPad rail stops at 834 px. These are artifacts of capturing fixed elements, not defects.
- DESIGN.md contradicts itself: its time-tile spec (a gold border on every open time, gold initials for someone else's hold) breaks its own Porch Light Rule. Worth settling in DESIGN.md before the redesign.

## 9. Provocative questions

1. Should the Schedule be two screens for two jobs? One would be a setter's "pick a time" board where open times lead; the other, Keenan's "my evening" run sheet where bookings lead and empty times recede.
2. The product is "built around the evening schedule", yet this screen never shows an evening. What if the times ran top to bottom in order, with the drive between meetings shown between them?
3. If gold means "the next thing to do", what is the one gold thing on a setter's screen at the door, and on Keenan's at 7:50 pm?
