## Overall impression

The board is the best thing on this tab:
- it is calm;
- it can be read at arm's length;
- it is clearly Vesper's.

The owner's tools say the right things about consequences. The trouble is where they say them: the most important warnings land below a fold, above the grid, or not at all.

The biggest opportunity is placement, not wording:
- put the consequences of a day change, holds above all, where Keenan cannot miss them;
- give his evening cards a clear order of actions.

## Design specificity verdict

**Specific at the centre, stock at the edges.**

**The board is authored for this business.**
- A strip of evenings counts what is open ("6 open", "No times").
- The heading names the evening: "Tonight", or "Saturday, November 14".
- Gold is reserved for open times. That is DESIGN.md's "Porch Light" idea, applied literally to a lighting company's dusk tool.
- Booked tiles lead with the neighbourhood, so setters can cluster Keenan's drive.
- A held tile shows who is booking it.
- Times past the cutoff say "Too late to book".
- Only Keenan sees "Blocked · tap to unblock".

These meanings are Vesper's. You could not drop this board into a salon booking app without rewriting it.

**The day-change pop-up is specific in what it says:**
- "What setters will see";
- times struck through with "Not offered: overlaps the 5:35 p.m. booking";
- "Stays at its own time";
- a summary by the buttons: "2 bookings stay where they are. 5:45 p.m. won't be offered."

**The periphery could belong to any app.**
- The meeting card is a stock appointment card: time, status, name and address, a row of outline buttons, a line of prose, then another row of outline buttons. Any CRM could ship it.
- The pop-ups are a standard bottom sheet on the phone, and a 448 px centred modal on the iPad, each holding a column of fields.
- The page header is stock: a title, a muted subtitle and buttons at the right.
- The loading screen shows three generic pulsing blocks, not the shape of the board.

**Missed character.** The board says little about the evening as Keenan lives it:
- where the gaps fall after a day change;
- which neighbourhood follows which;
- which leg between meetings is a long drive.

A tool for someone who drives between houses at dusk could carry that.

## Cognitive load

**Checklist**

- [x] **Single focus.** The board is the one thing on a setter's screen. Keenan's tools are two small outline buttons that don't compete with it.
- [x] **Chunking.** Times sit in rows of two (three on wide boards), the evenings in one strip, and the bookings in separate cards. One caveat: on a phone, a meeting card is one long block of 7 to 9 items.
- [x] **Grouping.** Mostly good. One miss: on the iPad, the evening tools sit above the bookings pane, about 700 px from the board they change.
- [ ] **Visual hierarchy.** **Fails inside Keenan's meeting cards.** Four outline buttons have equal weight, and the action he uses most at night, Navigate, is set smaller than Move and Cancel booking. The board itself passes: open times are clearly the brightest thing on it.
- [x] **One thing at a time.** Each pop-up holds one decision, and "All days" is a second step that confirms it.
- [ ] **Minimal choices.** **Fails** in three places:
  - Block time offers 5 choices in the phone capture, and 7 on a fully open evening;
  - the "Why not?" reasons number 5;
  - each card offers the lead link plus four buttons.
- [x] **Working memory.** The pop-up shows the current state and the result together, with the summary by the buttons. One small demand: on the phone, after jumping to a card, the only way back to the board is to scroll.
- [x] **Progressive disclosure.** The owner tools sit behind two buttons, the reasons appear only after "Not qualified", and the "All days" confirmation is a second step.

**Result:** 2 failures, so **moderate** cognitive load in Keenan's view. A setter, who sees only the board, has none (**low**).

**Decision points with more than 4 visible options**

- **Block time:** "The whole evening" plus every open time.
  - 5 options in the phone capture;
  - 7 on a fully open usual evening;
  - up to 13 on an evening with 12 meetings.

  The full-width "whole evening" row above a two-column grid of times chunks this well. It is the number of options that grows, not the layout that fails.
- **The outcome form's "Why not?":** 5 reasons from Settings (Not the homeowner, Decision maker missing, Didn't want a price, Roofline we can't do, Other), and Keenan can add more.
- **Each meeting card:** the lead's name link, Call, Navigate, Move and Cancel booking, all at near-equal weight. After the meeting starts: 4 outcomes plus Save outcome.
- **The evening strip:** 15 evenings, with about 4 visible on a phone and about 10 on the setter's iPad. They are in date order and each shows a count, so the load is low in practice. Listed for completeness.
- **At the limit but not over:** the day-change footer (Cancel, All days, Just this day), plus the "Use the usual times" link when the evening has its own times, makes 4.

## Emotional journey

**Setter, at a door, 4 to 6 pm**

- **Arrival: confidence.** The open times are the brightest thing on the screen, and the instruction under the title says what to do.
- **Peak: the tap.** The tile says "Holding", then the sheet opens with "Sat, Nov 14 at 5:00 p.m. held for you". The time is theirs, in front of the homeowner. The whole product is built around this moment, and it lands.
- **Valleys:**
  - Tapping a time a teammate holds answers in bold red above the grid. In front of a homeowner, red reads as failure.
  - In a dead zone, a stale board offers times that may already be gone, and the setter only finds out at the tap.
- **End:** back on the board, their booking sits quietly in grey, marked "Your booking". That is satisfying, but the tile does nothing when tapped, so it can't be used to manage the booking.

**Keenan**

- **Afternoon planning, on phone or iPad: reassurance.** The strip shows the two weeks at a glance and the tools stay quiet. The day-change preview lets him see consequences before he acts, and the summary by the buttons is the high point of the owner tools.
- **High-stakes moments: mostly well supported.** "All days" and "Cancel booking" ask first and state the consequence, and the block button names its count.
  - The gap: the warning that a setter's hold will let go is the easiest one to miss. It is also the one consequence that hurts someone else in real time.
- **Evening, on the phone, 6 to 9 pm: friction.** His bookings are a scroll away, each card is long, and Navigate is no easier to hit than Cancel booking.
- **End: silence.** Pop-ups close and the board redraws. After "All days", nothing confirms that Settings and the other evenings changed. The peak-end rule says the end should be spoken: one line naming what changed.

## What's working

**1. The board's states are specific, and carried by words as well as colour.**

| State | How it reads |
|-------|--------------|
| Open | Gold border and the word "Open" |
| Held by someone else | Dashed border, their initials, "Zane is booking" |
| Booked | Quiet border, muted time, the neighbourhood first, then who booked it |
| Past the cutoff | "Too late to book" |
| Keenan's extras | A chevron on booked tiles, and "Blocked · tap to unblock" |

- Each time is set at 24 px bold with tabular figures and a smaller "p.m.", readable at arm's length at dusk.
- The tiles are 80 px tall, so they are easy thumb targets.
- The heading that names the evening ("Tonight") removes any doubt about which day is on screen.

**2. The day-change pop-up shows consequences before the save.** It is the most considered piece of owner UI in the app.
- "What setters will see" lists the evening as it will be, and strikes through times that won't be offered, saying why.
- The summary by the buttons states the result in one sentence.
- Both saves stay off until the bookings check matches the numbers on screen; "Checking bookings" shows meanwhile.
- "Just this day", the narrower change, is the gold primary. "All days" is secondary, and asks first, listing the evenings that keep their own times.

**3. Restraint and copy.**
- Setters never see Keenan's tools, and his are two small outline buttons that don't compete with the board.
- Buttons say what they will do: "Block 4 open times", "Block 6:45 p.m.", "Keep it blocked", "Change all days".
- Empty states teach: "No meeting times this evening. Use the pencil to give this evening its own times."
- Failures give a next step.
- On the iPad, a sticky board beside the bookings is the right two-pane split.

## Priority issues

### [P1] The day-change pop-up can hide, or skip, its own warnings

**What**

- **The warnings are below the fold.** In "Times, [evening]", the warnings box (bookings that won't line up, holds that will let go) comes after the preview list, so it sits below the fold inside the pop-up:
  - on the phone, in the bottom sheet;
  - on the iPad, in a 448 px column on a 1194 px screen. The warning capture shows only the box's top edge above the footer.
- **The summary leaves out holds.** The summary by the buttons covers kept bookings and dropped times, but never holds. Its wording also counts a booking that still lines up as "staying where it is", so "3 bookings stay where they are" hides which ones no longer line up.
- **One save path has no check.** "Use the usual times for this evening" saves on tap. It shows no preview, runs no bookings check and gives no warnings.

**Why it matters**

- Keenan is most likely to change tonight's times between 4 and 6 pm, exactly when setters are holding times at doors.
- A hold that lets go mid-conversation breaks the promise the setter just made to the homeowner, that the time is theirs. The setter may already be on the sidewalk taking photos.
- No double booking is the product's core promise. This pop-up is the one tool that can take a held time away, and it is the one place where that warning is easy to miss.

**Fix**

1. Put holds in the summary, by name: "Zane is holding 6:45 p.m. That hold lets go." Name the bookings that won't line up: "5:35 and 6:10 stay at their own times."
2. Show the warnings box above the preview, right under the number fields, so it sits in view on a phone.
3. From 768 px wide, widen the pop-up to about 720 px and place the fields and the preview side by side, so nothing scrolls on the iPad.
4. Make "Use the usual times for this evening" load the usual numbers into the fields, so the same preview, check and summary run. The primary button then reads "Use the usual times".

**Suggested command:** /impeccable harden, then /impeccable adapt for the iPad pop-up.

### [P2] Keenan's evening on the phone: bookings start below the fold, and every card gives four actions equal weight

**What**

- **Below the fold.** On a 390 x 664 phone, the strip, heading and six tiles fill the first screen, and the "Bookings" heading sits behind the tab bar.
- **Long cards.** Each card is about 300 to 400 px tall and stacks:
  - the lead, then "X's lead";
  - the distance from the meeting before;
  - Call and Navigate, at 14 px;
  - a line of map-position prose;
  - Move and Cancel booking, at 16 px.
- **Inverted priority.** That makes four identical outline buttons, and the two Keenan needs on the road are set smaller than the two he rarely uses.
- **Long drives don't stand out.** A leg over the 10 km setting reads exactly like "Under 100 m".

**Why it matters**

- The same card is Keenan's Tonight on Home.
- Between meetings at dusk he needs Navigate and Call at a glance. The flat hierarchy makes him read four labels on every card.
- A long leg that eats a 35-minute slot doesn't stand out.

**Fix**

1. For Keenan only, add a one-line jump link with a count under the board heading: "3 bookings, see them below".
2. In the card, make Call and Navigate a full-width pair at 16 px.
3. Move Move and Cancel booking to a quieter row at the foot of the card, still 44 px tall.
4. Give a leg over the distance setting the design system's long-drive treatment: a neutral border and a bold first line.
5. Aim for a card under 300 px on the phone.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable distill for the card, then /impeccable layout.

### [P2] Tapping a time someone else holds answers in error red, above the grid, and moves the tiles

**What**

- "Zane is booking this time." appears as a bold red alert between the evening heading and the grid.
- It pushes every tile down about 36 px, or 56 px if it wraps to two lines.
- It stays until the next tap, even after Zane lets go and the tile turns gold again.

**Why it matters**

- This is information, not an error. DESIGN.md keeps red for errors only.
- The setter is often showing the screen to a homeowner, and red reads as "something broke".
- The shift lands under the thumb. A second tap can hit the open time above and place a hold on it, opening the sheet on the wrong time.

**Fix**

1. Say it in the tapped tile. Its detail line already reads "Zane is booking", so briefly emphasise that line, or use a floating notice that doesn't push the grid.
2. Use neutral styling, and keep red for real refusals.
3. Clear the note when the tile's state changes on the next read.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable clarify, then /impeccable polish.

### [P2] The board goes stale without saying so

**What**

- The time picker reads the board again every 3 s, and quietly ignores a failed read. The code comment says "No signal: the next read tries again".
- With no signal, the gold "Open" tiles go on showing the last good read indefinitely.

**Why it matters**

- Weak signal is one of the conditions PRODUCT.md names.
- A setter at a door can offer a homeowner a time a teammate took a minute ago, and only finds out at the tap.
- The database prevents the double booking. The awkward moment in front of the customer is for the design to prevent.

**Fix**

1. Track the time of the last good read.
2. After two failed reads (about 6 to 10 s), show one neutral line under the evening heading: "Not updated for 40 seconds. Check your signal."
3. Clear it on the next good read. No red, and no spinner.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable harden.

### [P3] Block time opens on the biggest action, and has no quick way back

**What**

- The pop-up opens with "The whole evening" already chosen, so the gold button reads "Block 4 open times" before Keenan picks anything.
- A whole-evening block is saved as one block per run of open times, split around bookings and holds.
- Each run is unblocked in its own pop-up, and there is no undo.

**Why it matters**

- A hurried tap blocks every open time during knocking hours.
- Undoing it takes one tap and one confirmation per run.
- The button's label makes the mistake unlikely, but the cost of recovery is high compared with the cost of the action.

**Fix**

1. After a block, show a short notice: "Blocked 4 times. Undo".
2. When Keenan opens a blocked time that came from a whole-evening block, offer "Unblock the whole evening" as well.
3. Or open the pop-up with nothing chosen, so the button reads "Pick what to block" until he taps a choice.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable harden.

## Minor observations

**Tiles and the board**
- **Uneven heights.** A booked tile with a third line is about 91 px tall, while open tiles in the same row stay at 80 px, on both phone and iPad. Make the tile fill its grid cell so rows line up.
- **Two forms of the same line.** "By TEST Setter A" (no neighbourhood) and "Booked by TEST Setter A" (with one) say the same thing two ways. Pick one; on Keenan's phone, "By Zane" or a first name would fit.
- **Gold initials.** The holder's initials sit in a gold circle on a time nobody else can take. It is the one place gold marks something that isn't the next action, and DESIGN.md's own Porch Light Rule says that when two golds compete, one is wrong. A Night Text circle would keep the initials legible without the pull.
- **"Full" when blocked.** The chip says "Full" for an evening that is entirely blocked, and Keenan may read "Full" as booked.
- **Clipped chips.** The strip cuts its edge chips mid-word ("d, Nov 11 / 5 open" on the iPad). Scroll snapping would keep chips whole; fades are out, since gradients are banned.

**Cards**
- **Different sizes.** Call and Navigate are links at 14 px with 16 px side padding; Move and Cancel booking are buttons at 16 px with 20 px padding. Same family, two sizes.
- **Repeated "Booked".** The status "Booked" repeats at the top right of every upcoming card. It earns its place only once outcomes vary.

**Header and layout**
- **The pencil.** It is the only icon-only control on the screen. Its spoken name is good, but a visible "Times" label would remove the guess. The spec names "the pencil", so this is Keenan's call.
- **Subtitle on the phone.** Keenan's header subtitle wraps to two lines because of the tools, and for him it is the least useful text on the screen.
- **Date twice on the iPad.** "Saturday, November 14" (board) and "Bookings, Saturday, November 14" (pane) repeat the date side by side; "Bookings" alone would do.
- **Loading screen.** It shows three 112 px pulsing blocks. A board-shaped skeleton (a strip and tile outlines) would make the arrival seamless.

**The pop-ups**
- **Time field.** The native time field shows "05:00 PM", while every other time in the app reads "5:00 p.m.". On iOS it will look different again, and it is a different kind of control from the two number fields beside it.
- **Disabled saves.** A disabled "Just this day" at 50% gold reads as a dim primary button, and before any change nothing says why both saves are off.
- **Unblock copy.** "Unblocking opens these times" is used even for a single time.

**Not verified in the captures**
- **Light theme contrast.** In the light theme, an open tile's gold border on white is about 2.9:1, under the 3:1 guide in WCAG 1.4.11 for a component's edge. The word "Open" still carries the state, but in daylight the board's main signal weakens.

**Worth keeping**
- The bookings pane dims while an evening switches, and Keenan's tools wait until it has loaded. That stops anything acting on the old evening.

## Questions to consider

1. What if Keenan's phone Schedule were one timeline for the evening, each time a row with its booking expanded in place, so the board and the cards stop being two stacked screens?
2. Should the two tools sit on the evening heading ("Saturday, November 14", then Block time and the pencil) so they are attached to the evening they change on both phone and iPad? The spec puts them in the page header, so this is Keenan's call.
3. Should a day change be able to drop a time someone is holding at a door at all? Or should a held time survive until it is booked or let go, with the new times fitting around it, as bookings already do?
4. Should a setter's own booking on the board open it (Move, Cancel), the way Keenan's booked tiles do?
5. For Keenan, would "3 booked" on each evening chip answer his question faster than "3 open"?
6. What would a confident end to a day change say? For example: "Saved. Saturday: 6 meetings of 45 minutes from 5:00 p.m. Settings unchanged."

## Findings

### B1. straggler (advisory, quality), phone, owner view. Verdict: real, low impact

- **Where:** `http://localhost:3300/app/schedule?day=2026-11-14`, selector `#meeting-75c7dc61-1af4-46a3-9598-bc5644015473 > p.text-sm:nth-of-type(1)`.
- **Detector:** `"meeting" alone on the last of 2 lines (18% of the measure)`.
- **Element:** the distance line of the 5:35 p.m. meeting card, `components/schedule/meeting-card.tsx` lines 102 to 106.
  - `nth-of-type(1)` picks the card's first direct `<p>` child.
  - The time and status `<p>`s sit inside a wrapper `<div>`, and the lead block is a `<div>`.
  - So whenever the distance line renders, it is that first `<p>`.
- **In the capture:** `phone-owner-1-schedule-full.png`, 5:35 p.m. card, shows "Under 100 m in a straight line from the 5:00 p.m." with "meeting" alone on the next line.
  - "meeting" measures 52 CSS px on a 324 px line, which is 16 to 18%.
  - Line 1 has about 21 px to spare.
- **Why it is real:** the break isn't intended, and it shows on the owner's phone, where he checks tonight's meetings. It is cosmetic, so advisory is the right level. The line always ends with the short word "meeting", so the break can come back whenever the line wraps on a phone.
- **iPad:** the line fits on one line there (both distance lines in ipad-owner-1 fit), which matches the empty iPad result.

#### Does the `text-pretty` change fix it?

It puts the right remedy on the right element, but nobody has checked the result, and it may swap the lone word for a time split across two lines.

- **Right element.** Commit 0c94dd7 adds `text-pretty` to this exact `<p>` (meeting-card.tsx line 103).
- **Right remedy.** It is what the rule recommends.
  - Tailwind 4.3.3 maps `text-pretty` to `text-wrap: pretty`.
  - The current build's CSS has `.text-pretty{text-wrap:pretty}`.
  - Its server chunk renders the distance line with `className:"text-sm text-pretty"`.
- **Not verified.** The scan ran before the change, and nothing has been rescanned since.
  - The rule works from where the last two words actually render (the engine's capture script measures them), so only a rescan with a screenshot settles it.
  - The local build (`.next`, built 05:35:55) already includes the change, so a rescan of the phone owner view needs no rebuild.
- **Possible side effect: a split time.**
  - `formatTime` returns "5:00 p.m." with an ordinary space before "p.m.". I checked this in Node 22 with ICU 77.1; the bytes are `35 3a 30 30 20 70 2e 6d 2e`.
  - On this same tab, Chromium's `pretty` has already split a time this way. In the phone Times pop-up (`phone-owner-4-day-times-warning.png`), the row "Not offered: overlaps the 5:35 p.m. booking" also has `text-pretty`. It renders as "...overlaps the 5:35" with "p.m. booking" on the next line.
  - From the captures, " p.m." measures 29 CSS px and line 1 had about 40 px free. Normal wrapping would have kept "p.m." on line 1 and left "booking" alone, so it was `pretty` that moved "p.m." down.
  - The distance line has the same shape, so it may now read "...from the 5:00" with "p.m. meeting" on the next line. The advisory would clear, since the last line now holds two words, but the time would be split.
  - Whether `pretty` moves one word or two here depends on the line lengths. The rescan screenshot will show which.
- **Unsupported browsers.** Browsers that don't support `text-wrap: pretty` keep the old break. On Apple devices, that means Safari before version 26.
- **What would work in every browser:** no-break spaces inside the time and before "meeting", so "5:00 p.m. meeting" moves as one piece. The rule's own text suggests binding the last two words with a no-break space; this extends that to the time.
