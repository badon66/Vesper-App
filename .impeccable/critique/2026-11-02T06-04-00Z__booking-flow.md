## Design specificity verdict

**Verdict: written for Vesper, inside a common frame.**

The frame is a standard mobile step-by-step flow: progress at the top, one screen per step, Back and Next at the bottom. Any product could use that frame.

Very little inside it would fit another product unchanged:

- **The held-time chip.** It turns a database lock into something the setter can say out loud: "Sat, Nov 14 at 5:00 p.m. held for you".
- **Step names and notes from the door process.** The steps are At the door, After the door and Book, and Keenan's own note for each step sits behind "?".
- **Let go and Keep holding.** The quiet row is placed so that a slip near Next can only pick another time. It can never release the hold (the comment in `BottomBar` says this was deliberate).
- **The soffit question.** It explains itself in terms of track colour: "White is the only stock track colour, so off-white matters."
- **"Use where I'm standing".** It reads the GPS accuracy back to the setter.
- **The time tiles.** They are the same tiles as on Schedule, including the holder's initials.

The look is the existing Porch Light system, applied carefully:

- one gold thing per area of the screen (Next, the held chip, the open tiles);
- chosen answers shown light on navy with a tick;
- flat surfaces and borders;
- Manrope set large on the phone.

It is restrained rather than distinctive. The character comes from how the flow behaves and what it says, more than from the layout.

**Missed chances.** A homeowner is most likely to look at two moments, and they are the plainest in the flow:

- The done screen is one sentence over empty navy.
- On the iPad, the device a homeowner reads most closely, the sheet is the phone column centred in a field of navy, with 12 to 14 px labels.

The detector scan is Assessment B's job and is not part of this assessment.

## Overall impression

This is a mature, carefully built flow:

- the hold is visible from the first tap to the last;
- the bottom bar never moves;
- every At the door and After the door screen fits a 664 px Safari phone without scrolling;
- the copy is in Keenan's words.

The weak spots are about timing and place, not looks. The flow splits the job into "at the door" and "on the sidewalk", but nothing closes off the door step:

- the phone number is only checked after the setter has walked away;
- so are the answers a booking needs;
- the house is never checked against the team's existing leads.

**The biggest opportunity:** make leaving the door a checkpoint, and check the house as well as the person.

## What's working

### 1. The hold reads as a promise

- **The chip.** It sits at the top left of every screen, in gold, and reads "Sat, Nov 14 at 5:00 p.m. held for you". It turns muted with "not held" after a lapse, and shows a tick and "booked" at the end. It is the one status that matters at the door, placed where the eye lands first, and it is the sentence the setter says to the homeowner.
- **Let go is guarded.** It asks first, and "Keep holding" is the gold button at the bottom right. The message says exactly what happens to the person ("... stays saved as a lead. Anyone can then pick this time."), with a one-tap way to let go and set a follow-up day.
- **A lapse explains itself.** The notice says why: the time is still free, someone is booking it now, or it was booked. It offers "Hold it again" only when that can work.
- **Small safeguards.** The screen stays awake while a time is held, and the draft comes back after an iPhone reloads the app following the camera.

### 2. The bars never move, and they are built for a thumb

- **Three fixed areas.** Gold Next always sits at the bottom right and Back at the bottom left.
- **Let go is out of the way.** It sits above Back, so the only thing near Next is Pick another time, which keeps the hold.
- **Answers are one tap.** Each is a 48 px button, a second tap clears it, and nothing moves on by itself, so the conversation sets the pace.
- **Nothing scrolls.** Every At the door and After the door screen fits without scrolling at 390x664 (Safari with its bars showing) and on the iPad, per both scroll records.

### 3. The words and the help come from the business

The step names are the door process. The "?" opens Keenan's own note for that step in a panel along the bottom, with Close at the bottom right.

The hints give real reasons in the setter's terms:

- "White is the only stock track colour, so off-white matters. Not sure is fine."
- "Map position from this phone at the door, accurate to about 10 m."
- "The phone's position is only accurate to about 62 m. Step away from the house, wait a few seconds and try again." (source)
- "Needed to confirm the meeting."

A new setter can learn the job from these screens, and no generic product could ship this copy.

## Priority issues

### 1. [P1] What has to happen at the door is only checked after the door

**What.** The flow itself says At the door happens with the homeowner and After the door happens on the sidewalk. But every check on door-only information runs after the door:

- **The phone number.** It is only checked when the lead is first saved, which is when Next leaves the address screen on the sidewalk. The message is "That phone number doesn't look right. Check the digits." with Go there. New lead checks the number when the field loses focus (`onPhoneBlur`); the sheet's `WhoScreen` doesn't, and checks run only in `saveSheetLead`. (source)
- **Owns the home and Wants a price.** Both must be Yes to book, yet they look exactly like the optional questions beside them. Only a No or Not yet says anything. An unanswered one stays silent until Book.
- **Leaving the door.** Tapping Next on "Will everyone who decides be there?" runs no check at all.

**Why it matters.**
- The setter finds out on the sidewalk, from Book's checklist, that the phone is a digit short or that Wants a price was never asked.
- The only fixes are to knock again, or to tap Fix and answer from memory.
- Answering from memory quietly breaks Keenan's rule that every booked meeting is a solid lead, and it is the easier path.
- The phone number is the one thing that has to be right to confirm the meeting.

**Fix.**
- Check the phone on the Who screen the way New lead does: as soon as the field loses focus, with the message under the field and the typed digits kept.
- Add one muted line under the Find out, 1 of 2 heading: "Owns the home and Wants a price need a Yes to book." The phone already has a line like this ("Needed to confirm the meeting."), and the screen has about 70 px to spare at 664 px.
- Turn the last At the door screen into a checkpoint. Under the everyone toggle, add a neutral box that lists anything from the door that is still missing, for example "Still to ask: phone number, wants a price". Each item jumps to its screen. Next stays enabled, since nothing is required and nothing moves on by itself. When everything is in, say so in one line: "Everything from the door is in."

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`, then `/impeccable clarify` for the hint and checkpoint wording.

### 2. [P1] Booking a house that already has a lead raises no warning

**What.**
- **Picking an address.** On New lead, picking an address calls `findLeadsAtPlace` and shows "This address already has a lead", with who has it. On the sheet's address screen, picking only stores the choice, so it is never checked against existing leads (`onPicked` in `booking-sheet.tsx`).
- **"Use where I'm standing".** This is the only duplicate warning in the sheet: `AddressField`'s 25 m nearby check, which runs for this button only.
- **That warning's style.** It is gold (`border-primary bg-accent`). DESIGN.md says a warning that doesn't block gets a neutral border, and the Porch Light Rule keeps gold for the next action.
- **That warning's link.** The link to the other lead changes the page underneath the full-screen sheet. The tap seems to do nothing, and when the sheet later closes the setter lands on that lead's page instead of where they started. (source)

**Why it matters.**
- The sheet is now how a booked door lead gets created; New lead is for quick follow-ups.
- Setters share neighbourhoods, and the team view exists so they don't knock the same doors twice.
- Without the check, a second setter can create and book a copy of a colleague's Follow up lead. The result is two leads for one house, an argument over whose booking fee it is, and Keenan finding out on the doorstep.

**Fix.**
- When an address is picked in the sheet, run the same check as New lead and show the same message: who has the lead and its status.
- Style it like the Book screen's distance warning: neutral border, bold first line.
- Inside the sheet, open the other lead in a small read-only pop-up (name, status, who added it, when) instead of a link that changes the page underneath.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`, with `/impeccable polish` for the warning's style.

### 3. [P2] Weak signal at the curb: uploads freeze the sheet, and the chip can overstate the hold

**What.**
- **Uploads freeze the bottom bar.** While any photo is uploading, Back, Next ("Uploading photos"), Let go and Pick another time are all disabled, and the step names can't be tapped. The upload has no time limit, so a stalled one holds the bar until the request itself gives up. Escape on a keyboard, or Android's back button, still reaches the Let go question; an iPhone has no way out. (source)
- **The chip stays too sure of itself.** A check-in that fails for lack of signal is ignored. The chip goes on saying "held for you" even after the 2-minute grace period has passed since the last check-in that worked. (source)

**Why it matters.**
- PRODUCT.md lists weak signal as a normal condition, and photos are taken on the sidewalk.
- A setter who wants to go back and fix the soffit answer, or whose homeowner has changed their mind, has to wait for an upload they can't cancel.
- Meanwhile the one promise on the screen, the held time, may no longer be true.
- The spec says photos upload in the background; here they hold the screen.

**Fix.**
- Keep Next disabled until the photos are up (or let Book wait for them), but leave Back, Let go and Pick another time working.
- Keep track of uploads outside the photo tiles, in the sheet itself. Then leaving the photos screen doesn't lose an upload, and Book still learns when each photo lands.
- Give each upload a time limit that ends in the existing "didn't upload, tap to try again" message.
- After a failed check-in, have the chip say "checking the hold" with a muted dot. Once the grace period from Settings has passed with no successful check-in, have it say "may have let go: no signal". On the next successful check-in, show "held for you" again, or the lapse notice.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### 4. [P2] The Book screen makes the last step harder than it needs to be

**What.**
- **List order.** Before you book is a flat list of seven items that doesn't follow the screens. Everyone, the last At the door screen, comes last, after Map position.
- **Button label.** When something is missing, the gold button is disabled and reads "Fix 2 first". The shape brief asked for "2 things missing" beside a button that still names the time.
- **No date.** The heading ("Ready to book?") and the button ("Book 5:00 p.m.") never name the day. The day appears only in the chip at the top left, far from the thumb.
- **Empty photo screen.** Tap "Book" in the progress bar from At the door, then Fix on photos. You get "Photos of the house" with no photo slots and only "Can't get the photos?", because the slots only appear once the lead is saved. (source)

**Why it matters.**
- Book is where the setter commits to a date with the homeowner, and where Keenan's rules bite.
- A list in a jumbled order makes "what's left?" slower to read.
- The only gold thing on the screen is a disabled button with a terse label.
- "Book 5:00 p.m." won't catch a wrong evening. That is easy to pick from a strip of 15 evenings, or from Home on a night with no meetings, where the times shown are tomorrow's.

**Fix.**
- Group the list under the progress bar's step names, in screen order:
  - At the door: phone, owns the home, wants a price, everyone;
  - After the door: map position, soffit, photos.
  Each group then has four items or fewer.
- Put the count in the list heading ("Before you book: 2 missing"). Keep the button saying what it will do ("Book 5:00 p.m."), disabled until everything passes.
- Make the heading the full time, as the shape brief proposed: "Book Sat, Nov 14 at 5:00 p.m.?", or "Book 5:00 p.m. tonight?". At 390 px the button has no room for the day.
- On the photos screen with no saved lead, say why and what to do: "Photos attach once they're saved. Add the address first." with Go there.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`, then `/impeccable clarify`

### Decision points with more than four visible options

- **Picking a time.** Six tiles an evening with Keenan's settings (up to 12), under a strip of 15 evening chips: about four visible on a phone, seven to ten on an iPad. This is acceptable: the options are all alike, in time order, and the task really is "pick one together". The line "No meetings tonight. This is the next evening with an opening." helps the setter find their place.
- **Before you book.** Seven items. It is a status list rather than a choice, but it can show up to seven Fix buttons. Group it by step (issue 4).
- **The bars around every screen.** The chip, "?", two or three tappable step names, Let go, Pick another time, Back and Next: eight controls framing one screen's content. The visual weight keeps them out of the way (one gold button, a quiet row, step names styled as labels), but that is still a lot of standing chrome for a one-task screen.
- **Find out, 2 of 2.** Ten answer buttons on one screen, although each question has four or fewer.

## Emotional journey

1. **The tap (high stakes, homeowner watching).** The tile says "Holding", then the sheet opens with "Sat, Nov 14 at 5:00 p.m. held for you" in gold at the top left. This is the high point of the flow, and it is earned: the setter can say "it's yours" and the screen agrees.
2. **At the door.** Light work. One-tap answers, nothing moves on by itself, and the "?" holds the script. One small dip on an iPhone: on the Who screen the keyboard covers Next, and the "next" key does nothing (source).
3. **Leaving the door.** No moment at all. The riskiest handover in the flow gets the same Next as every other screen, with nothing to reassure the setter that everything needed from the homeowner is in (issue 1).
4. **On the sidewalk.** The low point.
   - Address and GPS are handled well: the accuracy is stated, and the errors say what to do.
   - Then come photos in the dark, with uploads that freeze the bar on weak signal (issue 3).
5. **Book.**
   - Satisfying when all seven ticks show and the gold button names the time.
   - Deflating when it reads "Fix 2 first" and the missing item is something only the homeowner could answer.
6. **The end.** "Booked for Sat, Nov 14 at 5:00 p.m." over a calm, empty screen. Correct and on brand. But people remember a flow by its high point and its end (the peak-end rule), and this is a soft ending: it gives the setter nothing to say back to the homeowner if they are still there.

**Reassurance where it counts:**
- Let go asks first, Keep holding is the main button, and the message says what happens to the person.
- A lapse comes with its reason and Hold it again.

These are the right moves at the right moments.

## Minor observations

**Book screen and copy**

- Book shows only the name of each missing item. The plain-language fix lines already written in `lib/schedule/readiness.ts` (for example "Pick the address from the suggestions, or use where you're standing at the door.") never appear, so "Map position" has to explain itself.
- The lapse notice names only the time ("Your hold on 5:00 p.m. let go."), while the chip names the day. With 15 evenings in the window, use the chip's wording.

**Layout**

- With "Booking [name]" above it, Find out 2 of 2 is about 10 px too tall for a 664 px phone, so it scrolls. In `phone-sheet-15` the last row ends at 533 px, and the content's 20 px bottom padding runs past the bar at 543 px. The scroll records only cover the new-person path, which doesn't show the name.
- When the sheet opens from a lead, an empty three-step progress bar and "No time held yet" sit above the time picker. The bottom-right spot, which means "forward" everywhere else, holds an outlined Close.
- On Pick another time, the progress bar keeps showing the step you came from (At the door, three quarters full) above a grid of times.
- "Existing lead instead" sits on its own row between the heading and the fields, about 50 px on a phone. It could share the heading's row.

**What a booking records**

- The everyone confirmation has no No. A setter who hears "my husband decides and he's away" can only leave it unticked, and the reason the lead didn't book is lost.
- Choosing "Other" for the soffit opens "Which colour?", but nothing asks for it again before booking. Keenan can get "Other" with no colour.

**iPad**

- The type is phone-sized throughout: 12 px step names and photo-tile labels, 14 px question labels and chip.
- The photo hint says "phone".
- The evenings strip cuts chips off at the column edges.
- The device rules allow this, since the sheet is designed phone first. But this is the screen a homeowner reads most closely.

## Questions to consider

- What if leaving the door were its own moment: one line that says "Everything from the door is in", or names what's still to ask, before the setter turns away?
- Should the sheet check the house at the door rather than on the sidewalk? A quiet position check when the sheet opens could say "Jordan already has a lead here" before the setter offers a time.
- When the house is a colleague's lead, what does Keenan want: book it for them (their fee), or let the time go and hand it back? The sheet should say so in one line.
- What would the iPad sheet look like if it were designed for two readers: the held time large on the homeowner's side, the steps on the setter's?
- Should the done screen give the setter the sentence to say ("Keenan will see you Saturday, November 14 at 5:00 p.m."), instead of describing where the booking shows up?
- If uploads really ran in the background, would the photos screen need to hold the setter at all, or could Book simply wait for the last photo?
